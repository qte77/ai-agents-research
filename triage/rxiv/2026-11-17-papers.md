### Graph-Mediated Large Language Model Systems in the Agentic Era: Reasoning, Knowledge, Retrieval, Execution, and Coordination

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05953>
- summary: This survey introduces a taxonomy for understanding the role of graph operations in large language model systems and provides an evaluation framework for assessing their effects.
- methods: taxonomy development, comparison of graph operations, evaluation framework
- findings: importance of distinguishing graph operation functions; need to evaluate graph mechanisms across components and stages

### TAMPAgent: Agentic Belief-Space Planning for Mobile Manipulation

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03584>
- summary: The authors propose TAMPAgent, a framework that combines Large Vision-Language Model agents with Task and Motion Planning systems to enable open-world robotic mobile manipulation with partial information.
- methods: agentic planning, belief-space planning, classical search algorithms, constraint satisfaction
- findings: TAMPAgent outperforms direct tool-calling agentic baselines in complex household mobile manipulation tasks; integrating TAMP constraint satisfaction into agentic planning loops is essential for unstructured, partially observable environments

### Towards a Trustworthy and Governable Internet of Agentics: An Architectural Perspective on Protocols, Systems, and Services

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03066>
- summary: This perspective paper examines the architectural problem of dependable deployment of large language model applications as tool-using and multi-agent workflows, requiring interoperability across frameworks and organizational control domains.
- methods: protocol stack analysis, systems abstractions review
- findings: need for interoperability across frameworks and control domains; importance of governable service participants; reinterpretation of classical SOA and microservices concerns at the level of cognitive capabilities

### Agent-R$^2$: Scaling Agents for Realistic Productivity Work via Real-Source Data Synthesis

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08219>
- summary: The Agent-R$^2$ framework is introduced to scale training data for realistic productivity work, improving the performance of large language models in agentic reasoning and tool use.
- methods: supervised fine-tuning, rubric-based reinforcement learning, data synthesis
- findings: Agent-R$^2$ improves end-to-end productivity performance and file-grounded reasoning; Agent-R$^2$ mitigates shortcut behavior and promotes sustained exploration; Agent-R$^2$-4B surpasses Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 on GDPval and OfficeQA benchmarks

### MA-Mesh: Resilient Multi-Agent Collaboration via Role-based Self-Healing

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06389>

- methods: Cross-agent reflection, Role-based failover
- findings: MA-Mesh significantly outperforms existing multi-agent systems in both task performance and robustness under system uncertainty

### A Layered View of LLM Support in Underwater Robotics

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06162>

- findings: LLMs can support debugging, operator interaction, and post-mission analysis; LLMs are not yet suitable for direct control in critical applications due to limitations in grounding, consistency, and causal reasoning

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04813>
- summary: The authors introduce AgenticFinBench, a benchmark of 230 real-world financial tasks, and a reference-free evaluation framework to assess financial agents' performance and reduce hallucinations.

### Drift No More: Spectral Locking for Long-Horizon Open-Weight Multi-Agent AI

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04315>
- summary: The ATLAS method is introduced to address task drift, role drift, and state staleness in long-horizon multi-agent systems by steering agent hidden states toward their shared high-coherence eigendirections.
- methods: ATLAS, prompting, retrieval, memory, activation-steering
- findings: ATLAS outperforms baselines in long-horizon memory and state maintenance tests; ATLAS improves memory macro-average accuracy and reduces write/read failures; ATLAS gains points under perturbation and reaches high role consistency

### Do Agents Still Follow the Rules? Benchmarking Rule Compliance under Agent Context Compaction

- arxiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03223>
- summary: ARCBench is introduced as a benchmark to measure rule compliance in LLM agents after compacting long histories, revealing that most violations occur while the rule is still in the compacted history.
- methods: benchmarking, compaction configuration, adversarial settings
- findings: most violations occur while the rule is still in the compacted history; compaction strategy and budget decide whether the rule survives; retaining a rule does not guarantee compliance

### ComplianceAtlas: A Taxonomy-Driven Agentic AI Framework for Automated AI Act Gap Analysis and Remediation

- findings: The framework achieves a gap-type F1 of 0.831; The approach outperforms baseline methods with a remediation P@5 of 0.872
